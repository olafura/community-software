# Contributing

Thanks for being here. You don't need permission to help, and you don't need to write code. If you make the project better in any way, you are a contributor.

## Find your way in

We label issues so anyone can find a starting point:

| Label | Good for |
| --- | --- |
| `good-first-contribution` | Anyone new. These are small tasks, and someone is ready to help you through them. |
| `needs-reproduction` | Testers. Confirm a bug on your setup and add the steps that trigger it. |
| `docs-help` | Writers. Make a guide clearer, fill a gap, or fix the page that tripped you up. |
| `needs-design-feedback` | Designers and everyday users. Tell us how something feels to use. |
| `translation` | Translators. Bring the project to people in their own language. |
| `accessibility` | Anyone who uses or knows assistive technology. |

Not sure where you fit? Open a discussion and tell us what you enjoy doing. We'll help you find something.

## Reporting a bug

A good report is a real contribution. Please include:

- what you did, what you expected, and what happened instead
- your version and platform
- the smallest set of steps that shows the problem

Before you open a new issue, search for an existing one. Adding your details to an existing issue often helps more than a new one.

## Suggesting a feature

Describe the problem before the solution: what are you trying to do, and what gets in the way? Then tell us how you could help. You could write a spec, test early versions, document it, or build it. Ideas that come with an offer to help move the fastest.

Maintainers may say "no" or "not yet" to keep the project focused. That isn't a judgment of you or your idea.

## Making a change

1. For anything bigger than a small fix, open an issue first so we can agree on the approach before you spend time on it.
2. Fork the repository and create a branch.
3. Keep each pull request focused on one thing.
4. Explain what you changed and why. Link the issue it addresses.
5. Be patient. Reviews happen when volunteers have time.

Reviewers comment on the work, not the person. If something in a review feels unclear or unkind, say so. We'd rather fix the misunderstanding.

## Your first contribution

Everyone was new once. Asking questions is welcome, so is a draft pull request, and so is "I'm stuck". If English isn't your first language, don't worry about perfect wording. We care about the idea.

## Taking a break

You can step back at any time without explanation. If you're working on an issue and can't continue, a quick comment lets someone else pick it up. That's all.
