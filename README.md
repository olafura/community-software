# Community Software

**Software we build together, in the open.** · [Visit the website](https://community-software.org)

We're not a company and you're not a customer. We're people who care about the same tools, sharing the work of making them better. Here's how we keep that fun and sustainable.

## Seven things we agree on

1. **Everyone is welcome.** You belong here whatever your background, identity, language, ability or experience. Beginner questions are good questions. Write in plain language, assume good faith, and remember that many people here are working in their second or third language.

2. **We are peers.** This software is free to use, change and share. It comes with no warranty and no service agreement. Nobody here owes anyone free work, quick fixes or particular features. People give their time because they care about the project, so treat that time as a gift, not an obligation.

3. **Every contribution counts.** Code is one part of a healthy project. Documentation, triage, translation, design, accessibility review, testing pre-releases and answering questions all matter just as much. If you help the project in any way, you are a contributor.

4. **Influence grows from participation.** Decisions are guided by the people who do the work, not by whoever is loudest in a thread. Participating doesn't need to be big. You can reproduce a bug, suggest a clearer paragraph, or help test a proposal. Feedback is always welcome. It carries the most weight when it comes with an offer to help.

5. **Be kind to people, rigorous with ideas.** Honest technical discussion makes the software better. Personal attacks make the community worse. Point out edge cases and question trade-offs. Don't belittle people, use sarcasm to make a point, or chase maintainers across channels.

6. **"No" and "not yet" are valid answers.** To keep the project maintainable, maintainers sometimes decline features or close pull requests that don't fit its scope. A declined idea is not a rejection of you. Because the code is open, you're always free to build an extension or a fork.

7. **Look after yourself and each other.** Open source moves at the pace of real lives. Don't expect weekend replies or instant reviews. Your health, family and life come first, for maintainers and contributors alike. Stepping back for a while is normal and welcome.
