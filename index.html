A community of peers

Software we build together, in the open.

We're not a company and you're not a customer. We're people who care about the same tools, sharing the work of making them better. Here's how we keep that fun and sustainable.

The charter

Seven things we agree on

  1. 01

    Everyone is welcome

    You belong here whatever your background, identity, language, ability or experience. Beginner questions are good questions. Write in plain language, assume good faith, and remember that many people here are working in their second or third language.

  2. 02

    We are peers

    This software is free to use, change and share. It comes with no warranty and no service agreement. Nobody here owes anyone free work, quick fixes or particular features. People give their time because they care about the project, so treat that time as a gift, not an obligation.

  3. 03

    Every contribution counts

    Code is one part of a healthy project. Documentation, triage, translation, design, accessibility review, testing pre-releases and answering questions all matter just as much. If you help the project in any way, you are a contributor.

  4. 04

    Influence grows from participation

    Decisions are guided by the people who do the work, not by whoever is loudest in a thread. Participating doesn't need to be big:

    • Reproduce a bug, or add the details that make a report useful.
    • Suggest a clearer paragraph where the docs confused you.
    • Write up a proposal, help test it, or open a pull request.

    Feedback is always welcome. It carries the most weight when it comes with an offer to help.

  5. 05

    Be kind to people, rigorous with ideas

    Honest technical discussion makes the software better. Personal attacks make the community worse.

    Do
    Point out edge cases, question trade-offs, and explain how a problem affects your work.
    Don't
    Belittle people, use sarcasm to make a point, or chase maintainers across channels to bump your issue.
  6. 06

    “No” and “not yet” are valid answers

    To keep the project maintainable, maintainers sometimes decline features or close pull requests that don't fit its scope. A declined idea is not a rejection of you. Because the code is open, you're always free to build an extension or a fork.

  7. 07

    Look after yourself and each other

    Open source moves at the pace of real lives. Don't expect weekend replies or instant reviews. Your health, family and life come first, for maintainers and contributors alike. Stepping back for a while is normal and welcome.

Ways in

There's a place for you, whether or not you code

We label issues so anyone can find a good starting point. Pick the one that sounds like you.

  • good-first-contribution

    New here?

    Small, well-described tasks with someone ready to help you through them.

  • needs-reproduction

    Testers

    Confirm a reported bug on your setup and add the steps that trigger it.

  • docs-help

    Writers

    Make a guide clearer, fill a gap, or fix the page that tripped you up.

  • needs-design-feedback

    Designers & everyday users

    Tell us how something feels to use. Sketches and screenshots welcome.

  • translation

    Translators

    Bring the software and its docs to people in their own language.

  • accessibility

    Accessibility

    If you use or know assistive technology, your eyes on our work are invaluable.

Keeping it healthy

Small habits that make the charter real

A friendly note on every issue

Just above the submit button on every issue form:

A short, fair process when things go wrong

If someone isn't following this charter, write to us. A small group of volunteers reads every message in confidence.

conduct@community-software.org
  1. A conversation. Usually a private, friendly note is all it takes.
  2. A formal warning. If the behavior continues.
  3. Time away. A temporary, or in serious cases permanent, removal from project spaces.

This charter belongs to all of us. If something here could be clearer or kinder, open a pull request.
